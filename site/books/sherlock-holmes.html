The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes: The Red-Headed League

By Sir Arthur Conan Doyle

A mysterious gentleman visits 221B Baker Street with an extraordinary story about a league of red-headed men and a bizarre job copying the Encyclopædia Britannica.

Chapter 1: The Strange Client

I had called upon my friend, Mr. Sherlock Holmes, one day in the autumn of last year and found him in deep conversation with a very stout, florid-faced, elderly gentleman with fiery red hair.

With an apology for my intrusion, I was about to withdraw when Holmes pulled me abruptly into the room and closed the door behind me.

'You could not possibly have come at a better time, my dear Watson,' he said cordially. 'Mr. Jabez Wilson here has been good enough to tell me a story which promises to be one of the most singular that I have listened to for some time.'

  • Cordially [/ˈkɔː.di.ə.li/] - In a warm and friendly manner (Az: Səmimiyyətlə, nəzakətlə)
  • Singular [/ˈsɪŋ.ɡjə.lər/] - Remarkable, extraordinary, unique (Az: Fövqəladə, qeyri-adi)

Chapter 2: The Red-Headed League

'Well, Watson,' said Holmes, 'it appeared in the morning paper two months ago: TO THE RED-HEADED LEAGUE: On account of the bequest of the late Ezekiah Hopkins, of Lebanon, Pennsylvania, U.S.A., there is now another vacancy open which entitles a member of the League to a salary of 4 pounds a week for purely nominal services.'

Mr. Wilson explained how his assistant Vincent Spaulding encouraged him to apply, and how he was accepted into the office where his sole duty was to copy out the Encyclopædia Britannica from nine until two every day.

Then, one Saturday morning, a notice was pinned to the door: 'THE RED-HEADED LEAGUE IS DISSOLVED. October 9, 1890.' The entire office was empty, and the league had vanished without a trace.

Key Vocabulary in this Chapter

  • Bequest [/bɪˈkwest/] - A legacy or property left in a will (Az: Vəsiyyət, miras)
  • Vacancy [/ˈveɪ.kən.si/] - An unoccupied position or job (Az: Vakansiya, boş iş yeri)
  • Dissolved [/dɪˈzɒlvd/] - Officially closed down or ended (Az: Ləğv edildi, bağlandı)

Comprehension Questions (Q&A)

Chapter 3: The Cellar at Coburg Square

Holmes examined the pavement outside Jabez Wilson's pawnbroker shop by tapping his walking stick against the stones. He noticed Vincent Spaulding's trousers were worn and stained with dirt at the knees.

'Watson,' whispered Holmes, 'a huge crime is being prepared. Tonight we shall catch one of the coolest and most daring criminals in London.'

That night, waiting in the dark vault beneath the City and Suburban Bank, Holmes and Watson sprang upon the infamous John Clay just as he broke through the cellar floor to steal thirty thousand Napoleons in gold bullion!

Key Vocabulary in this Chapter

  • Pawnbroker [/ˈpɔːnˌbrəʊ.kər/] - A person who lends money on the security of personal property (Az: Lombardçı)
  • Bullion [/ˈbʊl.jən/] - Gold or silver in bulk before coining (Az: Külçə qızıl)
  • Infamous [/ˈɪn.fə.məs/] - Well known for some bad quality or deed (Az: Bədnam, adı çıxmış)
