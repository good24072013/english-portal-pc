The Little Prince (Illustrated English Reader)

By Antoine de Saint-Exupéry

An aviator stranded in the desert meets an extraordinary young prince from Asteroid B-612 who teaches him profound lessons about love, friendship, and seeing with the heart.

Chapter 1: The Aviator and the Desert

Six years ago, I had an accident with my airplane in the desert of Sahara. Something was broken in my engine. And as I had with me neither a mechanic nor any passengers, I set myself to attempt the difficult repair all alone. It was a question of life or death for me: I had scarcely enough drinking water for a week.

The first night, then, I went to sleep on the sand, a thousand miles from any human habitation. I was more isolated than a shipwrecked sailor on a raft in the middle of the ocean.

Thus you can imagine my amazement at sunrise when an odd little voice woke me up. It said: 'If you please—draw me a sheep!'

Key Vocabulary in this Chapter

  • Scarcely [/ˈskeəs.li/] - Barely, only just (Az: Çətinliklə, güclə)
  • Habitation [/ˌhæb.ɪˈteɪ.ʃən/] - The state of a place being lived in (Az: Məskunlaşma, yaşayış)
  • Amazement [/əˈmeɪz.mənt/] - A feeling of great surprise or wonder (Az: Təəccüb, heyranlıq)

Comprehension Questions (Q&A)

  1. Why was the aviator stranded alone in the desert?
  2. What was the very first request made by the little voice?

Chapter 2: Asteroid B-612 and the Rose

The little prince lived on a planet scarcely larger than a house: Asteroid B-612. On his planet there were baobab trees that threatened to tear the soil apart if they were not pulled out when they were young.

And on his planet, there was also a flower—a proud, delicate rose with four thorns. She demanded water, a glass globe to shield her from the wind, and constant admiration.

'I ought not to have listened to her,' the little prince confided to me. 'One never ought to listen to the flowers. One should simply look at them and breathe their fragrance. Mine perfumed all my planet, but I did not know how to take pleasure in all her grace.'

Key Vocabulary in this Chapter

  • Delicate [/ˈdel.ɪ.kət/] - Fragile, easily broken or harmed (Az: Zərif, incə)
  • Fragrance [/ˈfreɪ.ɡrəns/] - A sweet or pleasant smell (Az: Ətir, xoş qoxu)
  • Confide [/kənˈfaɪd/] - To share a secret in trust (Az: Sirr vermək, etibar etmək)

Comprehension Questions (Q&A)

  1. Why did the little prince need to pull out young baobabs?
  2. What did the flower require from the little prince?

Chapter 3: The Secret of the Fox

It was then that the fox appeared under the apple tree.

'Come and play with me,' proposed the little prince. 'I am so sad.'

'I cannot play with you,' said the fox. 'I am not tamed. To tame means to create ties. To me, you are still nothing more than a little boy who is like a hundred thousand other little boys. And I have no need of you. And you, on your part, have no need of me. But if you tame me, then we shall need each other. To me, you will be unique in all the world.'

When the time came to say goodbye, the fox gave the prince his secret: 'And now here is my secret, a very simple secret: It is only with the heart that one can see rightly; what is essential is invisible to the eye.'

Key Vocabulary in this Chapter

  • Tamed [/teɪmd/] - Brought into a state of trust and friendship (Az: Əhlilləşdirilmiş, yaxınlaşmış)
  • Essential [/ɪˈsen.ʃəl/] - Absolutely necessary, most vital (Az: Əsas, ən vacib)
  • Invisible [/ɪnˈvɪz.ə.bəl/] - Unable to be seen by the eyes (Az: Gözəgörünməz)

Comprehension Questions (Q&A)

  1. What does 'to tame' mean according to the fox?
  2. What is the famous secret that the fox reveals to the prince?
